**Two of these overlap us directly and it is worth being honest about which:**

- **VolAlpha is `alpha/brains/vol_gap.py`.** IV-vs-realised around scheduled
  events, on Alpaca, through MCP. Our vol brain is not a differentiator against
  this team; the *shape → instrument* layer above it is.
- **midas-gate independently reached "engine computes, LLM narrates."** The
  deterministic-envelope-before-the-model pattern is not ours alone. What is
  still ours is that the envelope is a **capability boundary** (`ALPACA_TOOLSETS`
  withholding `trading`, so the model has no order verb) rather than a
  convention someone could edit.

## What actually won before

Both from lablab's AI Agent Olympics / Kraken trading track, May 2026.

**Kraken Alpha Agent — winner.** xStocks ranking, guardrails, Vercel dashboard,
judge quickstart. Headline: **+$19.18 across 10 trades, 80% win rate, 0.06% max
drawdown** over the 13–19 May window — **and it was a backtest**, because live
xStocks was blocked at the venue.

**Falcon — second-order causal reasoning** ("NVDA beats → who benefits next?" →
ARM/TSMC/AMD). Live: **$10,058 portfolio, +$58.84 after fees (+0.58%)**, 4
positions, Flask + JS dashboard, solo build.

**The P&L bar is low.** A winner's headline was 0.19% on a backtest. Five
sessions of real paper fills at our risk profile clears that easily. P&L is
criterion 1 and it is winnable — which means the *other* three criteria are
where the ranking is actually decided.

## The two judge comments that should shape the submission

**On the winner:**

> *"The transparency note is the strongest signal here. Calling out that xStocks
> live was blocked at the venue level, and showing that the engine still produced
> live fills on the crypto diagnostic, **separates this from teams that pretend
> everything worked.** Risk posture is the right one too: dry-run default, triple
> opt-in, 1.0x leverage, reduce-only exits. The backtest sample is small (10
> trades inside the window)... Auditability framing is a strong angle to keep
> developing."*

**On Falcon:**

> *"The presentation stayed entirely at the idea level. I kept waiting to see how
> the reasoning actually worked in practice. **Show me one real news event, show
> the chain of reasoning, show why ARM was chosen over five other correlated
> companies, and show whether the trade ended up being right.** Without that,
> it's hard to tell if the system is genuinely reasoning or **just generating
> plausible financial narratives after the fact.**"*

Four things follow, and they are observations about a rubric rather than guesses
about taste:

1. **An admitted failure outscored a claimed success.** The transparency note was
   named the strongest signal in the winning submission. So the write-up says
   what did not work — the $99 feed we did not buy and what it costs us, the
   brains we did not finish, the sessions we lost money.
2. **One worked example beats an architecture diagram.** One symbol, one
   catalyst, the chain we saw, the eight structures, the one we took, and the
   outcome.
3. **"Why this and not the five alternatives" was asked for explicitly.** That is
   `alpha/counterfactual.py`, and it exists because of this sentence.
4. **Post-hoc narrative is the specific failure judges name.** Anything the LLM
   says has to be checkable against a number recorded before the outcome.

## What we keep, and what we drop

**Keep — because they are measured, not asserted:**

1. **Shape → instrument.** A TAIL signal *is* an option; a STEP signal is a
   stock book. The agent refuses to buy convexity on `profit_roe` despite it
   carrying the strongest statistical evidence in the parent project (ic_t 4.18)
   because its decile curve is a plateau. No prompt-based agent makes that
   refusal, and it comes out of 32 years of CRSP replay rather than a prior.
2. **MDM before EV.** A structure bought at the ask and sold at the bid breaks
   even at a specific underlying move; unless our distribution puts materially
   more mass beyond it than the chain's, we agree with the market.
3. **The counterfactual/abstention screen.** Literally what a judge asked for.
4. **Capability-boundary safety.** 44 tools exposed to the model instead of 72;
   `place_option_order`, `place_stock_order`, `close_all_positions` and
   `exercise_options_position` are not in its list.

**Drop for this week, with reasons:**

- **Expectation Gap engine** — needs a news/estimates pipeline we do not have and
  cannot validate in five sessions.
- **Causal relay / second-order** — this is *Falcon's* idea, and Falcon was
  marked down for exactly the failure a five-day version would have.
- **Internal capital market between brains** — needs ≥4 independent brains. We
  have one. Building the market before the brains repeats the parent project's
  diagnosed bottleneck in a new costume.
- **Tournament-aware sizing** — already built (`alpha/engine/sizing.py`).

## Honest note on novelty

Post-rejection counterfactual sampling has prior art — arXiv 2606.08228 and
2607.02830 audit filter-rule rejections in DEX trading and find 17.9% of rejected
events would have taken a major loss. It is not a new idea. It is new *in this
field*, on options, and it is the specific artefact a judge asked a previous
entrant for.
